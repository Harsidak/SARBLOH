\begin{abstract}
\begin{tintbox}
ARC-AGI-3 measures fluid intelligence through interactive games that an agent has never seen, scored by how few actions it needs compared with a human. In such unknown environments, perceiving the game state correctly is the first thing an agent needs. Yet when frontier models are shown raw $64{\times}64$ integer grids, they spend hundreds of actions rediscovering mechanics that a human grasps at a glance. We present \textsc{Sarbloh Agent}, an agent built around a deterministic host-side perception engine and a code-as-action runtime. In $\approx$35\,ms per frame, the engine produces three aligned views: an object-centric state with identity tracking across steps, a briefing that separates verifiable geometric invariants from affordance hypotheses paired with one-action tests, and an annotated spatial map. The agent writes Python world models and action plans over these views, supported by an append-only context that reuses 98.4\% of the prefix cache and a priority scheduler that shares one GPU across 25 concurrent games. In a perception lab of eight presentations, the structural facts the engine computes carried over exactly to 23 of 23 unseen games, while role guesses made from a single frame did not, which is why the engine states the first as facts and the second as guesses with a test. On one RTX PRO 6000, \textsc{Sarbloh Agent} clears 143 of 183 public levels in 8.2 hours and scores 16.53 on the hidden Kaggle evaluation, up from 10.98 in the preceding build.
\end{tintbox}
\end{abstract}
