\begin{abstract}
ARC-AGI-3 evaluates fluid intelligence of AI or AI based systems by utlizing interactive game and how few actions it needs, 
relative to a human, to master such unknown environments AI has never seen. Such enviornments which are unknown to LLM, understanding or perception
becomes crucial. While LLMs can learn to play games with a few-shot prompt, they struggle with spatial grounding. When SOTA models are shown raw $64{\times}64$ integer grids,
it burn hundreds of actions rediscovering mechanics a human grasps at a glance. We present \textsc{Sarbloh Agent}, an agent built around a deterministic host-side perception engine and a code-as-action 
runtime. In $\approx$35\,ms per frame, the engine produces three aligned views: an object-centric state with identity tracking 
across steps, a briefing that separates verifiable geometric invariants from affordance hypotheses paired with one-action tests, 
and an annotated spatial map. The agent writes Python world models and action plans over these views, supported by an append-only
context that reuses 98.4\% of the prefix cache and a priority scheduler that shares one GPU across 25 concurrent games. In a 
controlled rubric, structured perception raises affordance identification from 3/28 to 25/28 while cutting input tokens by 
89.4\%. On one RTX PRO 6000, \textsc{Sarbloh Agent} clears 143 of 183 public levels in 8.2 hours and scores 17.01 on the hidden Kaggle
evaluation.
\end{abstract}
