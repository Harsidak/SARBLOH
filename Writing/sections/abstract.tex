\begin{abstract}
\begin{tintbox}
Sample-efficient fluid intelligence requires autonomous agents that rapidly form operational models of unfamiliar interactive environments. ARC-AGI-3 evaluates this ability across uninstructed visual games under Relative Human Action Efficiency, penalizing exploratory waste quadratically. When frontier reasoning models interact with raw integer grids, discrete serialization destroys spatial topology, inducing perceptual misinduction where models squander hundreds of actions rediscovering elementary boundaries and symmetries. We introduce \textsc{Sarbloh}, an autonomous open-weight agent architecture structured on the premise that spatial perception, rather than planning capacity, governs interactive reasoning on ARC-AGI-3. A zero-parameter, $\mathcal{O}(HW)$ perception engine reconstructs frames in 35\,ms into an object inventory with bipartite temporal tracking, a geometric briefing separating proven invariants from affordance hypotheses under the Measured--Guessed Rule, and an annotated spatial canvas. Paired with a speculative code-as-action runtime, the agent synthesizes algorithmic hypotheses rather than low-level motor primitives. Across eight structural presentations, grounded perception elevates benchmark rubric acquisition from $3/28$ to $25/28$ points ($8.3\times$) while reducing token consumption by $89.4\%$. On twenty-three held-out games, geometric invariants generalize unconditionally with $100\%$ spatial accuracy, whereas single-frame affordance guesses drop to $\le 50\%$, confirming the necessity of active hypothesis verification probes. On cleared public levels, \textsc{Sarbloh} achieves a median human action ratio of $0.77\times$, with error decompositions attributing seventy-two percent of unearned score loss to perceptual misinduction on stalled environments. Within the nine-hour competition budget on a single Kaggle NVIDIA RTX PRO 6000 GPU, \textsc{Sarbloh} resolves 143 of 183 public levels and achieves an official hidden Kaggle score of 16.53, advancing $13.7\times$ beyond the first Milestone winner.
\end{tintbox}
\end{abstract}
