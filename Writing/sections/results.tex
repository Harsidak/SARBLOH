\section{Results}
\label{sec:results}

Our empirical evaluation establishes that action budget exhaustion on ARC-AGI-3 is driven predominantly by perceptual misinduction rather than downstream planning failure. We first present the controlled experiments and out-of-distribution evaluations of the Perception Engine (Section~\ref{sec:lab_results}), followed by action efficiency and error budget decompositions (Section~\ref{sec:efficiency}), and conclude with hardware-matched comparative benchmarks and systems telemetry (Section~\ref{sec:benchmark}).

\subsection{Empirical Evaluation of the Perception Engine}
\label{sec:lab_results}

\paragraph{Controlled perception lab on development environments.}
To evaluate how alternative state representations influence visual scene comprehension, we designed a controlled perception lab evaluating nine distinct presentation formats on the opening frames of \code{ls20} (discrete arrow-key navigation) and \code{r11l} (continuous coordinate click interactions). Each presentation was graded against a pre-registered 28-point rubric measuring seven essential perceptual dimensions: agent identification, goal state, mechanical links, hazards, status gauges, terrain composition, and winning plan induction. As detailed in Table~\ref{tab:lab} and Figure~\ref{fig:lab}, our initial baseline (the Legacy Encoder, \code{arc\_eye}) scored only 3 of 28 points despite consuming 3,500 text tokens per frame. Because it serialized coordinates without relational synthesis, the reasoning model bore the entire burden of deducing spatial semantics, misclassifying functional move gauges as inert screen chrome.

Representational interventions advanced comprehension across two distinct axes. First, host-side geometric computations (X3 Visual Routines and X4 Look-alike Threads) extracted spatial properties that neural vision encoders fail to resolve reliably, such as discovering midpoint tethers and identifying that the \code{ls20} inventory icon matches the goal glyph rotated by $90^\circ$ and rescaled. Second, candidate role annotations (X7 Role Ledger) provided rapid operational context, raising the rubric score to 20/28. Synthesizing these approaches into the Fused Briefing (X8) achieved 25 of 28 points at 370 tokens per frame, representing an $8.3\times$ score improvement over the baseline alongside an 89.4\% reduction in token consumption. Dumping raw $64\times 64$ ASCII grids alongside per-cell component tables forces the tokenizer to spend $\approx 3{,}500$ tokens per frame encoding repetitive background coordinates---by abstracting connected components on the host, moving spatial layout into the visual Blueprint raster, and emitting only non-redundant geometric relations in text, the Fused Briefing compresses prompt footprint by 89.4\% (to 370 tokens) while increasing prefix-cache capacity by $9.5\times$.

\paragraph{Cross-distribution validation across 23 held-out games.}
Rubric scoring on the two development frames was conducted non-blind against a pre-registered answer key---out-of-distribution generalization is therefore verified programmatically across the 23 held-out games in Table~\ref{tab:holdout}. Ground-truth verification was executed programmatically against the official game engine without manual intervention. 
As reported in Table~\ref{tab:holdout}, deterministic coordinate reconstruction achieved 100\% cell accuracy across all 23 games ($23/23$). Furthermore, computed geometric invariants (4-connected segmentation, bounding enclosures, and dihedral symmetries) proved unconditionally invariant across novel layouts.

In stark contrast, single-frame operational guesses generalized poorly out of distribution. Candidate agent identifications were accurate on only 6 of 14 keyboard environments, moves-left gauge heuristics succeeded on 10 of 20 games, and click-affordance predictions held on only 7 of 29 candidate handles. Forward predictive simulation (X5) succeeded on only a single game out of 16. 
These empirical findings substantiate the \textbf{Measured--Guessed Rule}: mathematical invariants hold unconditionally by construction and should be asserted as ground truth, whereas latent operational affordances cannot be reliably deduced from static visual frames and must be framed as provisional hypotheses paired with unit-action verification probes.

\subsection{Action Efficiency and Error Decomposition}
\label{sec:efficiency}

\paragraph{Super-human action efficiency on resolved environments.}
On levels where the agent successfully induces environment mechanics, \textsc{Sarbloh} executes trajectories with high efficiency. Across 143 cleared levels in the 25 public games, the median ratio of agent actions to the human baseline ($a_l / h_l$) was \textbf{0.77}, with 66\% of cleared levels matching or outperforming the human baseline (Figure~\ref{fig:scorebudget}a). Under the RHAE metric, 62\% of completed levels earned the maximal score cap of 115\%. By synthesizing complete algorithmic search routines in Python and committing multi-step paths via \code{await play()}, the agent avoids incremental trial-and-error once the scene structure is understood.

\paragraph{Perceptual misinduction dominates the error budget.}
We decomposed the 40.6 total points lost from a perfect benchmark score using official RHAE weighting (Figure~\ref{fig:scorebudget}b). Unreached levels resulting from earlier stalls accounted for 20.1 lost points, initial uncleared levels contributed 9.2 points, and sub-optimal action overhead on cleared levels represented only 11.3 points. 
Consequently, 72\% of all lost points (29.3 of 40.6) occurred on environments the agent failed to resolve. Trace autopsies on these stalled environments reveal that failure was driven by perceptual misinduction rather than planning depth. On stalled levels, the agent reformulated its stated goal a median of 8 times (compared to once on cleared levels) and formulated incorrect inductive beliefs, such as declaring \code{vc33} unsolvable after 739 actions before squandering 1,240 further moves. Misperceiving latent rules blinds the search space, reinforcing that perceptual grounding is the governing operational bottleneck.

\paragraph{Official held-out Kaggle evaluation trajectory.}
Because the official Kaggle benchmark executes offline on undisclosed environments, it provides an uncontaminated measure of out-of-distribution reasoning (Figure~\ref{fig:leaderboard}). Initial baselines adapting Prime Agent with open weights scored between 0.00 and 0.27, repeatedly executing voluntary resets without finding a goal. Systematic architectural upgrades advanced performance to 5.14 through SGLang speculative decoding, 5.65 via unified \code{await play()} programmatic execution, 6.06 through deterministic context drainage, and 10.98 with persistent symbolic memory dictionaries. Integrating the full deterministic perception engine alongside refined drainage established our peak verified score of \textbf{16.53}, representing a $13.7\times$ efficiency improvement over the first Milestone winner.

\paragraph{Hardware-matched comparative baseline.}
To isolate algorithmic contributions from compute resources, we evaluated \textsc{Sarbloh} directly against the first Milestone winner (the Duck harness \citep{duck2026}) on identical Kaggle hardware (one NVIDIA RTX PRO 6000) and identical Qwen3.8-Flash-Next model weights. While the baseline ran four parallel passes per game (100 concurrent streams), \textsc{Sarbloh} executed a single scheduled pass across the 25 public games. As shown in Figure~\ref{fig:curves}, \textsc{Sarbloh} consistently accelerated discovery, clearing 73 levels versus 54 at one hour, 97 versus 63 at two hours, and finishing at 143 of 183 levels in 8.2 hours versus 129 distinct levels for the baseline. This efficiency stems from our priority scheduler concentrating compute on game states with high marginal progress potential rather than partitioning resources uniformly.

\paragraph{Comparison with frontier model baselines and systems telemetry.}
On public environments, \textsc{Sarbloh} achieves an aggregate mean score of 72.5 across evaluation trials, outperforming proprietary frontier models evaluated via unconstrained APIs on 21 of 25 games and achieving 100\% resolution on 13 games (Figure~\ref{fig:pergame}). Under a single-pass regime, \textsc{Sarbloh} achieves a mean score of 59.4, exceeding the strongest frontier baseline (GPT-6.1 Sol at 54.8). 
In terms of runtime telemetry on the single Kaggle RTX PRO 6000 GPU, the append-only context preserved a 98.4\% prompt prefix-cache hit rate across 1.78\,B total prompt tokens, ensuring that prefill consumed only 1.3\% of busy execution time compared to 50.1\% for decode generation. Across 21,224 model calls, the runtime suffered zero server failures (Table~\ref{tab:telemetry}). The comparison of this briefing-off run with the nearest briefing-on run is in Appendix~\ref{app:telemetry} (Table~\ref{tab:onoff}). Runtime failure modes and ablated designs are detailed in Appendix~\ref{app:negative_results}.
